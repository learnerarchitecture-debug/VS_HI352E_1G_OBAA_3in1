Theory of Shallow Foundations

1. Load Transfer Mechanism

The footing acts as a load-spreading membrane: the column/wall load P (concentrated) is redistributed over a larger soil contact area A, producing a bearing pressure q = P/A. Two conditions must hold:

  • Bearing capacity: q ≤ qall (no soil shear failure)
  • Settlement: total & differential settlement within limits (no structural damage)

The footing also resists erosion/uplift by its self-weight and depth of embedment, and transmits lateral forces through friction/adhesion at the base.

2. Bearing Capacity Theory (Terzaghi, 1943)

Ultimate bearing capacity of a strip footing on soil (general shear failure):

qult = c·Nc + q·Nq + 0.5·γ·B·Nγ

  • c = cohesion, q = γ·Df (overburden at base), γ = unit weight, B = footing width
  • Nc, Nq, Nγ = bearing capacity factors — functions of φ (angle of internal friction)

For square footings: qult = 1.3·c·Nc + q·Nq + 0.4·γ·B·Nγ
For circular: qult = 1.3·c·Nc + q·Nq + 0.3·γ·B·Nγ
For rectangular (B × L): shape factors applied (IS 6403 / Vesic).

Allowable bearing pressure: qall = qult / F.S. where F.S. = 2.5–3 (3 for permanent structures). The net pressure used in sizing: qnet = q applied − γ·Df.

3. Settlement Theory

Settlement = immediate (elastic) + consolidation (clay) + secondary creep:

Immediate: Si = (q·B·(1−μ²)/E)·If  (μ = Poisson's ratio, E = soil modulus, If = influence factor)

Consolidation: Sc = Σ (Δe/(1+e0))·H = Σ mv·Δσ·H  (clays only; from oedometer test)

  • Allowable total settlement ≈ 25 mm (frames), 50 mm (masonry walls / rafts); differential ≈ 10–19 mm
  • Settlement controls width B; bearing capacity controls the smaller of the two computed areas (take the larger area)

4. Strength Design Theory (RCC footings, IS 456 / ACI 318)

  • Bending moment: footing acts as an inverted cantilever — soil pressure (upward) × projection = moment at column face; Mu = qu·a²/2 per unit width (a = projection). Steel designed for Mu · factored loads (×1.5).
  • One-way (beam) shear: critical section at distance d from column face; τv = V/(B·d) ≤ τc,max (no shear steel allowed in footings ≤ 500 mm thick).
  • Two-way (punching) shear: critical section at d/2 from column face, perimeter b0 = 4(c+d); τv = V/(b0·d) ≤ k·τc (k = 0.5 + β, β = shorter/longer side ratio). Usually governs the depth!
  • Thickness h is therefore governed by shear, not flexure — typically h = 1.2–1.5× the flexural depth required.
  • Development length: bars must be anchored over the projection: Ld = (0.87·fy·Ø)/(4·τbd) ≤ projection − cover; else bend bars / provide 90° hooks.

5. Eccentric Loading — "Middle Third" Rule

When the load resultant falls within the footing at eccentricity e:

  • qmax = P/(B·L)·(1 + 6e/B),  qmin = P/(B·L)·(1 − 6e/B)
  • No tension rule: keep e ≤ B/6 with the resultant inside the middle third, otherwise the footing lifts off the soil (or use effective area method: B′ = B − 2e, AASHTO).
  • The middle-third rule is why edge columns use combined or strap footings (resultant kept central).

7. Key IS 456:2000 Clauses for Footings

  • Cl. 34.1: depth of footing may be reduced towards edges; min at edges 150 mm (on soil)
  • Cl. 34.2: design for bending at column/wall face; transfer of load by bearing + dowels
  • Cl. 34.2.4: shear — critical sections as described in theory 4
  • Cl. 34.3: minimum reinforcement 0.12% (Fe415) / 0.15% (Fe250); spacing ≤ 3t or 450 mm
  • Cl. 34.4: nominal cover 50 mm minimum for footings
